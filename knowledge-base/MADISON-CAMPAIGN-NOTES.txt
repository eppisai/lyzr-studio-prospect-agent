MADISON CAMPAIGN NOTES

These are the sales team's campaign choices. For what Madison does, use the Madison page.

WHO WE APPROACH
Banks and credit unions.

WHO FITS BEST
Madison is new and is recruiting a small group of charter institutions that will build it with us. That shapes who we approach first.
- Core fit: a bank or credit union approaching or recently past a regulatory size threshold, such as 10 billion dollars in assets, where examination expectations step up and the compliance team is still small. Also an institution of similar size with no dedicated GRC platform team.
- Stretch fit: a much larger bank that already runs a GRC platform with its own engineers. The timing can be strong, but a new product is unlikely to displace that platform soon. One honest message is worth sending. A full campaign is not.
- Outside: not a bank or credit union.

WHAT WE ASK FOR
To walk one obligation together, from the rule to the evidence behind it. A first message offers no price, pilot, delivery date or integration, and does not mention the charter program. Figures and sample records on the Madison page are illustrations. Do not quote them.

WHO IS LIKELY TO OWN THIS
Compliance leadership, usually the Chief Compliance Officer's office. Examination teams and risk and compliance analysts are the day-to-day users. Treat the named owner as unknown until a source names them.

WHAT EACH BUYER CARES ABOUT
These are working assumptions. Confirm them in the first conversation.
- Owner: compliance leadership. Being able to show an examiner or the board, for any obligation, the policy, the control and the evidence behind it.
- Sponsor: the executive that compliance reports to, often the Chief Risk Officer or General Counsel. Confidence that coverage holds across the whole bank, including after a merger.
- Day-to-day user: examination teams and risk and compliance analysts. The time spent assembling evidence before an exam, and keeping the links current when a rule changes.

WHAT MAKES AN ACCOUNT MORE RELEVANT
- A merger, acquisition or systems conversion.
- Crossing a regulatory asset threshold that brings new obligations.
- A new charter or license.
- A stated compliance, risk or controls program.
- A public enforcement action about controls. This informs the rating. It is never used as an outreach hook.

WHAT MAKES AN ACCOUNT LESS RELEVANT
- A named GRC platform already in place, or an in-house build. Ask about it. Do not rule the account out.

CLAIMS WE DO NOT MAKE
- That Madison certifies or guarantees compliance.
- Any ROI, time saving or customer outcome for Madison.
- Another Lyzr customer's result presented as a Madison result.
- Regulatory or legal advice.
